Maternal care coordination · Proof of concept

Bridging the distance between mothers and maternal healthcare.

Reach connects a mother's concern to a real clinician, connects that clinician to the right facility, and makes sure the next step in her care is not lost. It is designed to work on a basic phone as well as a smartphone.

The problem

Maternal care is a journey, and it breaks in five places.

These gaps are connected, so Reach is built as one system rather than five separate tools.

Communication

Mothers cannot reliably reach a provider between visits.

Several channels, including ordinary phone calls.

Continuity

Follow-ups and appointments are missed.

Care plans, attendance records and follow-up tasks.

Geography

Hard to know which facility can help, and how far it is.

A verified service directory and estimated travel, for clinicians.

Referral

A mother is told to go somewhere and nobody checks.

Issued, acknowledged, arrived, outcome: every step tracked.

Information

Providers lack one view of what happened.

One record of messages, decisions and follow-up.

How it works

One mother, one coordinated journey.

She experiences a single service. Clinicians and county teams each see a different view built for their job.

  1. She is registered at a clinic or by a community health worker, with her consent, preferred language and preferred channel.
  2. Routine messages and reminders reach her on the channel she can actually use.
  3. She raises a concern or asks for a call back, without needing airtime or a smartphone.
  4. A clinician reviews it. Automated suggestions help organise the queue. They never decide, and never dismiss a concern.
  5. The clinician chooses the pathway: a remote consultation, a scheduled visit, or in-person assessment.
  6. If she must be seen, Reach shows facilities with the verified service, and the referral is tracked until she is received.
  7. The outcome is recorded and the next follow-up is scheduled. The loop is closed.

Geography that does a job

Maps that help decide, not just decorate.

Reach keeps a directory of facilities and the services each one has been verified to offer. When a clinician decides a mother must be seen in person, the destination is chosen for the service it provides first, and distance second.

County teams see coverage and follow-up patterns by ward, with small counts hidden. Individual households are never shown on a dashboard.

Travel times are estimates, always labelled as such.

Clinician-led

The software suggests. A clinician decides.

◌ Automated suggestion

  • Helps organise and label incoming messages.
  • Says when it is not sure, and asks for a human.
  • Is kept separate from the clinical record.
  • Never reassures a mother, and never removes a concern from the queue.

✓ Clinical decision

  • Made and recorded by a verified clinician.
  • Confirms or overrides a suggestion, with a reason.
  • Chooses the pathway and the referral destination.
  • Every step is traceable.

Honest status

Where Reach is today.

A proof of concept built on synthetic data. There are no real users, no pilot and no results yet, and we would rather say so plainly.

✓ Built

  • Care-coordination data model, with geographic support
  • Consent checks before messages and referrals
  • Append-only records for messages, decisions and referrals
  • Clinician-only decisions, kept apart from suggestions

→ In progress

  • Clinician review queue and dashboards
  • Facility directory and referral board
  • Mother dashboard: visits, baby, booking with clinic availability
  • Community health worker view

○ Not yet

  • Real SMS and voice integration, including sign-in codes by text
  • In-app audio and video consultations
  • Mother documents and messaging with the clinic
  • Clinician review of the triage rules
  • A signed pilot facility
  • Data-protection review
  • Any real-user results

For partners and funders

Help us take Reach from proof of concept to a careful pilot.

The next steps depend on people who know maternal care better than software does.

  • A clinician to review the triage categories and escalation rules.
  • An introduction to a facility willing to run a small, supervised pilot.
  • Support meeting health-data and safety requirements.
  • Pilot funding that covers telephony and video, not only software.
